faq-q: What does "in" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: In is the word of the inside. It says that a thing is within a place, a time or a group: "the say is in the send" &mdash; the say is inside the send; "in the morning" &mdash; inside that time; "I am in the four" &mdash; I am one of them, inside the group. It is word 30 of the 850 &mdash; the next word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gave his acts their order in time; between gave him the middle space; by named the doer and kept the near thing near; down took him down the line to the low place; from said where the coming starts; in puts him inside.
faq-q: How does "in" work with the verbs?
faq-a: In goes before or after the word it works on. As a place-word: "I am in the send" &mdash; the send is about me and I am inside it. With put: "I put the say in the send" &mdash; the say is inside the send after my act. With come: "they came in" &mdash; they came from out to in. With keep: "they keep the say in" &mdash; the say does not go out of its place. With see: "I see him in the four" &mdash; he is inside the group of four, I see him there.
faq-q: What is the difference between "in" and "at"?
faq-a: At takes you to one point of a place: "I am at the send" &mdash; I am by it, at its side. In puts you inside the place: "I am in the four" &mdash; the group is about me, I am one of its parts. At is the word of the point; in is the word of the inside. You come at a thing and then you are in it: "I came at the group; I am in the group."
faq-q: What word comes after "in"?
faq-a: The next word is "off" &mdash; word 31 of the 850. In gives the hero the inside: the say in the send, the hero in the group. Off will show the taking-away: "I take it off; I go off."
