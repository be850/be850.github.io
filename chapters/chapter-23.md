faq-q: What does "among" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Among is the word of the middle of a group. A thing is among other things when it is in the middle of them, and they are all round about it. "I am among them" &mdash; they are round about me, and I am one of them. It is word 23 of the 850 &mdash; the fifth word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among puts the hero in the middle of them all.
faq-q: How does "among" work with the verbs?
faq-a: Three ways. Put it with a verb of going, and the road goes into the middle: "I go among them" &mdash; I take my place in the group. Put it with a verb of putting or keeping, and the group takes the thing in and holds it: "They put me among them; they keep me among them." Put it with a verb of saying, and the word goes from one to another through the group: "I say it among them." After keeps the line of acts; against pushes the line; among says where the hero now is &mdash; in the middle.
faq-q: What is the difference between "against" and "among"?
faq-a: Against makes two sides; among takes the sides away. "The send came against me" &mdash; two things met on the road, and each went its own way against the other. "I am among them" &mdash; there is no push and no line: they are round about me, and I am one of them. In the chapter the send comes against him (word 22), and then they put him among them (word 23): the push is over, and the group takes him in &mdash; which is a new kind of trouble, quiet and round.
faq-q: What word comes after "among"?
faq-a: The next word is "at" &mdash; word 24 of the 850. Among puts a thing in the middle of more than two; at puts a thing at one point and keeps it there: "I am at the door" &mdash; one place, one point, no group. The hero has his subject (word 19), his road (word 20), his order (word 21), his push back (word 22), and his place among them (word 23); the next chapter puts him at one point &mdash; and the hidden story will say what he sees there.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With against the push back had its say: "It is against you&hellip; You are the against." With among the group takes him in: they put him among them, the four were five, and they said "You are the among." The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: they kept me; it was me; it is me.
