faq-q: What does "come" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To come means to move toward the speaker or toward a place the speaker points to. It is word 1 of 850 and the first real word of the whole book.
faq-q: Why is Chapter 1 so short?
faq-a: Chapter 1 is allowed to use only one real word — come (plus pronouns, a and the). The book grows exactly as fast as you do: every next chapter adds one word, so the text gets longer as your English gets stronger.
faq-q: Why does the chapter end with "It comes!"?
faq-a: The it in "It comes" points to nothing you can see — English grammar demands a subject even when there is no actor. Grammarians call it the dummy it. The mystery of that invisible it is the hook of the whole story.
faq-q: Is "come" the same as "go"?
faq-a: No — they point in opposite directions. Come moves toward the speaker (Come to me!), go moves away (Go home!). Basic English separates them, and so does the story: in Chapter 1 the hero cannot go anywhere — the world can only come to him.
faq-q: Can I really learn English starting from one word?
faq-a: Yes — that is the idea of the book. Ogden's Basic English works with 850 words that cover about 90 percent of everyday speech. The novel feeds you those 850 words one at a time, in full sentences, so every chapter is readable the moment you open it.
faq-q: What is "the coming" in the last lines?
faq-a: It is the verb turned into a thing — a gerund. English lets a verb act like a noun: the coming comes, the getting gets. Basic English uses this trick to squeeze many meanings out of a tiny vocabulary, and the story uses it as a drumbeat.
