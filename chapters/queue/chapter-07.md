faq-q: What does "make" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To make means to create or to cause: make a thing, make it go, make me happy. It is word 7 of the 850 and one of the most productive verbs in everyday English.
faq-q: What does "I make me" mean in the story?
faq-a: The hero with the hole in his memory tries to build himself the way he builds things. It is the strangest sentence so far — and the engine of the plot: is a person something you get, something you are given, or something you make?
faq-q: Why does the chapter answer "It makes me"?
faq-a: Grammar and plot in one move. In English, make + person means cause them to act — it makes me come. The story reads the same words literally: something is making him. Who the maker is, is the question the chapter leaves open.
faq-q: What is the difference between "make" and "do"?
faq-a: Make creates a thing (make a cake, make a plan); do performs an activity (do work, do it again). English mixes them up constantly. In the book make is word 7 and do is word 12 — five chapters apart, like two sides of one coin.
faq-q: What forms does "make" take?
faq-a: make, makes, making and the irregular made. And the causative pattern makes the verb bigger than itself: make it go, make them come, make me get it — one verb, endless sentences.
