faq-q: What does "till" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Till says how long an act goes on: the say till the coming, the keep till the going. It is word 42 of the 850 &mdash; the next word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gave his acts their order in time; between gave him the middle space; by named the doer and kept the near thing near; down took him down to the low place; from said where the coming starts; in put him inside; off took a thing away; on put it back; over took it over; through took it through; to took the act to its end; under put the keep under the send; up took the say up from under; with keeps the say with him; as says the being of one in the place of the other; for says who the give is for; of says what the give is part of; till says how long the say goes on.
faq-q: How does "till" work with the other words?
faq-a: With keep: "I keep the say till you come for it" &mdash; the keep goes on the whole way up to the coming. With go: "the going goes on till the going of the send" &mdash; till gives the act its measure in time. With come: "the say goes till the coming" &mdash; the coming is where the going stops. With give: "I had it till the coming; at the coming I gave it to you" &mdash; till names the limit of the having, and the give comes after the limit. Till may stand before a name: "till the coming"; it may stand before a say: "till they came".
faq-q: What is the difference between "till" and "before"?
faq-a: Before says the order of two acts in time; till says how long one act goes on. "I said the say before the coming" &mdash; the saying came first and the coming came after. "I said the say till the coming" &mdash; the saying went on the whole way up to the coming and stopped there. Before puts two acts in a line, one after the other; till takes one act and gives it its end. In the chapter the hero keeps the say till the coming: the keep is one long act, and the coming is where it stops.

faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With of the fragments said what the say was part of. With till they say how long it went on: they made the say of me till the coming; they kept the keep of me till they came; the say of me was of the coming till the going. The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: the say was his till the coming came &mdash; and at the coming it was the me of them.

The send came before me; I was of the send till the coming. The say was of me; I kept it. I kept the say till the coming came.

The till says the going-on of the say. The say goes on till a coming; the keep goes on till a going. From the take-up of the say to the put-down of the say, the say goes on; the till says the coming of the going of it. It is the till of the say: the going-on comes to the coming; the coming comes to the say.

I came to the keep with the say of me. The keep was of them; I was of the say. I said to them: keep the say till I come for it. They said: we keep it till you come. I said: keep it till the going of the send; keep it till the coming of the coming. They said: we keep it till the coming comes.

The till says: the say goes till the coming; the keep goes till the going. I put the say of me down at the keep; I keep it till you come for it. The send goes on till the coming comes; the going goes on till the say is of me. I was of them till the coming; they were of me till the going.

The coming came. I went to the keep; I saw the keep of the say of me. I took the say up; I gave it on. The keep of it went on in them; the say of it went on in me. I was of the say as I was of them; the till of the keep was done.

A say till a coming is a keep; a say after the coming is a give. I had the say of it in me. I had it till the coming; at the coming I gave it to you. The give of it was of the say; the say of it was of me; the keep of it was of them.

The send of the going goes on; the send of the coming goes on with it. I see the coming before the coming comes; I keep the say till the coming comes. I will keep it; I will keep it till you say the say of me. May I keep it? I may; I will. The say of me goes till the coming; the coming goes till the say is of you.

The till says: the say goes till the coming; the keep goes till the going; I go with it till the coming of the say of you.
