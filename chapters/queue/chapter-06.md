faq-q: What does "let" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To let means to allow — to give permission for something to happen. It works in a special pattern, let + person + action: let me go, let it come. It is word 6 of the 850.
faq-q: Why is "let" a small word with power in the story?
faq-a: Until now things happened to the hero. With let he gets the steering wheel: he can allow the presence to come — or let it go. Permission is the first control he has, and the chapter is him testing it.
faq-q: Is "Let it go" a phrase I will hear in real English?
faq-a: Constantly — it is one of the most famous English phrases (and a famous song). It means: allow it to leave, stop holding it. The book gives you the phrase in its simplest possible surroundings.
faq-q: What does "It lets me go" mean grammatically?
faq-a: Let follows a fixed three-part pattern: subject + let + person + bare verb. No to is needed — it lets me go, never it lets me to go. The chapter drills that pattern from both sides.
faq-q: What is the difference between "let" and "make"?
faq-a: They are neighbours with opposite feelings: make somebody do something means cause it; let somebody do something means allow it. Make is word 7 — the next chapter — and the story is already setting up the pair.
