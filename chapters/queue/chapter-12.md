faq-q: What does "do" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Do is the doing word &mdash; the verb of action itself: to do a thing, to make it happen, to perform it. Its forms are do, does, doing, did and done. It is word 12 of the 850, and with it the book can finally ask questions and finish actions.
faq-q: Why does "do" make questions possible?
faq-a: In English, most questions are built with do: Do I go? Do they come? The verb steps in front and carries the grammar, while the main verb keeps its shape. Chapter 11 could state &mdash; it is me. Chapter 12 can ask &mdash; do I? A statement becomes a choice, and a choice becomes a plot.
faq-q: What is the difference between "do" and "make"?
faq-a: Make is do with a result: you make a thing, and the thing exists. Do is the action itself: you do it, and it is done. The hero makes the words, but he does the doing &mdash; chapter 12 is where acting becomes its own act.
faq-q: What does "It is done" give the story?
faq-a: Done is the past of doing &mdash; an act finished, sealed, unable to be undone. The fragment says it twice: I did it; it is done. Memory stops being a picture and becomes a deed the hero owns. What was taken from him now has an author: me.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With do comes did, and the hidden story turns from what they did to him to what he did first: I did it. The mystery moves from the cave to the hero himself.
