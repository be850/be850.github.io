faq-q: What does "keep" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To keep means to continue to have or to hold a thing: not give it, not lose it — hold it. It is word 5 of the 850 and the first verb of possession in the book.
faq-q: What is the difference between "get" and "keep"?
faq-a: Get is the moment of receiving; keep is everything after it. You get a thing once, you keep it for a long time. The story needs both: the hero gets a word — then fights to keep it.
faq-q: Why does "it keeps me" end the chapter?
faq-a: The sentence flips the roles. All chapter long the hero keeps things — then the unseen presence keeps him. English shows the flip by swapping the places of the two words, and the story shows it as a change of power. That is the cliffhanger.
faq-q: What forms does "keep" take?
faq-a: keep, keeps, keeping and the irregular past kept. Notice the family: sleep–slept, keep–kept. The book slips such families in one at a time, so the grammar arrives as a feeling, not as a rule to memorize.
faq-q: Why is the chapter built from so few words?
faq-a: Chapter 5 may use only words 1 to 5 of Ogden's list (plus pronouns, a and the). The narrowness is the design: with five verbs the sentences stay simple enough to read on day one, and the story still moves forward.
