faq-q: What does "be" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Be is the verb of existence and equality: to exist, to be a thing, to be somewhere. Its forms are am, is, are, was, were, being and been. It is word 11 of the 850 — and the most used word in the whole English language.
faq-q: Why is "be" only word 11 and not word 1?
faq-a: Because Ogden's list is ordered by the work a word does, not by how often it sounds. For ten chapters the verbs of action did the acting — and gave the story a plot. Now that things happen, be can state what is. Actions first, existence second: that is also the order a child's mind grows in.

faq-q: Is "It is me" correct English?
faq-a: In everyday English, yes — it is what almost everyone says. Formal grammar prefers It is I (the subject form after be), but Basic English and the book side with living speech. You will sound natural, not like a textbook.
