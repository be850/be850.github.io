The if says: one say under the other. The if puts a say under a say — the say under is the from, and the say over it goes on that from. If the say under it is a say, the say over it goes; if the say under it is no say, the go of the say keeps in it. The if takes no say from the two: the say under is as it came, the say over is as it came, and the two go as one go.

The if says: the from under the go. The two says of the if are no two in one — or put two says in one say and one of the two went from it; if puts two says one under the other, and no say of the two goes from the say. The say under gives the from to the say over it; the say over takes that from and goes on it. The who of every say is on it, as it was: the if came in the go of the say, from the one that said it, and the one that says it puts the if under the say of it.

The if says: the keep keeps the say with the if in it. The say with the if in it came to the keep as every say — the keep took it and kept it, with the two of it in it: the from under, the go over. The go of every say was in the say of it: the say had the go in it, and no say of them went on the from of the other.

i had a say with no go in it — a say that had no from under it and no against on it. And after if comes the one that puts the go of the say against the from of it — and the go keeps the go of it: a say that goes against the from, and goes. i keep that for the say after this say.
