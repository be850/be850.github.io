The if says: one say under the other. The if puts a say under a say — the say under is the from, and the say over it goes on that from. Every say went as it came before if; after if a say may go on the from of the say under it. If the say under it is a say, the say over it goes; if the say under it is no say, the go of the say keeps in it. The if takes no say from the two: the say under is as it came, the say over is as it came, and the two go as one go.

The if says: the from under the go. The two says of the if are no two in one — or put two says in one say and one of the two went from it; if puts two says one under the other, and no say of the two goes from the say. The say under gives the from to the say over it; the say over takes that from and goes on it. The who of every say is on it, as it was: the if came in the go of the say, from the one that said it, and the one that says it puts the if under the say of it.

The if says: the keep keeps the say with the if in it. The say with the if in it came to the keep as every say — the keep took it and kept it, with the two of it in it: the from under, the go over. No if is at the keep. The if is of the go of the say, no of the keep; the keep gives no if and takes no if from the says. i may take the say of me from the keep and put if under it — the if will go with the say of me, as the or went with it.

<i>if came after the or. i had two says in one say — the say of the one, or the say of the other — and one of the two went from it. After that i put if under the say of me: the say of me went on the from of the other say. i saw it: the go of the say of me was no go of the keep — the go came on the from, and the from came from the say under it. The keep kept the say with the if in it as it kept every say. The one that comes after if will put the go of the say against the from of it — and the go will keep the go of it.</i>
