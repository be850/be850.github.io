faq-q: What does "before" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Before is the word of time. It puts one thing in front of another thing: the first thing came first, the second thing came after it. "I saw it before I said it" &mdash; the seeing came first; the saying came after. It is word 25 of the 850 &mdash; the seventh word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gives his acts their order in time.
faq-q: How does "before" work with the verbs?
faq-a: Before takes two acts and puts the first act in front of the second. With a verb of seeing: "I saw them before they saw me" &mdash; the seeing came first. With a verb of saying: "I said it before I did it." With a verb of coming: "They came at me before the say" &mdash; the coming was first, the say came after. And with be: "It was before me; it will be before them" &mdash; the thing stands in front, in the line or in time.
faq-q: What is the difference between "after" and "before"?
faq-a: After and before are the two ends of one line of time. After puts an act behind another act: the send went, and the hero came after it (word 21). Before puts an act in front of another act: the send came before the say &mdash; the send was first, the say was second. "I went after the send" is a man who follows; "the send came before me" is the same line seen from its front end. One word looks back; the other looks from the front.
faq-q: What word comes after "before"?
faq-a: The next word is "between" &mdash; word 26 of the 850. Before is a word of time for two acts: one in front, one behind. Between needs two things with a space in the middle: one thing on this side, one thing on that side, and the between-thing between them. The hero has his subject (19), his road (20), his order (21), his push back (22), his place in the group (23), his point (24), and the order of his acts in time (25); the next chapter will ask what is between him and them.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With at they kept him at it and said "You are the at." With before the fragments give the order of that night: they came at him before the say; they said it before he saw it; he was at the one before the four came. They said: "You are the before. It was before you." The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: the order was there before he saw it; it was me.
