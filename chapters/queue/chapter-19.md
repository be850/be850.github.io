faq-q: What does "about" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: About is the word that goes round a thing. "A story about the cave" points at what the story holds inside it; "they say it about me" points at whom the saying stands. About is also the little word of "here and there": "I go about" &mdash; I move from place to place, with no road and no end in view. It is word 19 of the 850 &mdash; the first word of the list that is not a verb &mdash; and with it the book can point at a subject, not only at an act.
faq-q: How does "about" work with the verbs before it?
faq-a: Words 1 to 18 were acts: come, get, give, go, keep, let, make, put, seem, take, be, do, have, say, see, send, may, will. About adds no act of its own; it stands round an act and says what the act is round: the send was about something, the saying was about someone. In the story the hero gets the word for his own subject at last &mdash; everything they did, they did about him, and "It is about me" is the hero reading the title of his own book.
faq-q: What do "go about" and "come about" mean?
faq-a: Put about after a verb of moving and the move spreads out: "I go about" &mdash; I walk from place to place; "they go about" &mdash; they move here and there. "Come about" grows a step further and means "happen": "it came about" &mdash; it came to be. Basic English keeps its word-list small and lets the small words carry big loads; about is one of the heaviest carriers in the book.
faq-q: What word comes after "about"?
faq-a: The next word is "across" &mdash; word 20 of the 850. About rounds a thing where it stands; across looks to the other side: from this side to that side. The hero's going is fixed (word 18) and his subject is named (word 19); the next chapter gives his road its far side &mdash; and the hidden story, which sent him about a thing, will want the crossing.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With about the hidden story names its own subject: they sent him about it, and the it was him. The send had a purpose all along; the purpose was the sent. The book now has its title, and the title is about me.
