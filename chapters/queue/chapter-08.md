faq-q: What does "put" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To put means to move a thing to a place and leave it there. It is word 8 of the 850 — and the only verb of the first ten that is about position, not action.
faq-q: How can the chapter say "put" with no place words?
faq-a: On purpose. Words like down, in, on and up are numbers 28 to 37 of the list and have not been learned yet. So the chapter puts without naming places — and the strangeness becomes the story: he puts things, and nothing stays where it was put.
faq-q: What is the difference between "put" and "take"?
faq-a: They are two hands of the same action: take moves a thing to you, put moves it away from you to its place. Take is word 10 and comes two chapters later — the book pairs them so you feel the contrast instead of memorizing it.
faq-q: Why does "I get me!" return in this chapter?
faq-a: In Chapter 2 the hero could only ask "I get me?" — it was an open question. Here, after putting himself somewhere, he says it flat: I get me. The book resolves small mysteries one word at a time; that is its reading reward system.
faq-q: What forms does "put" take?
faq-a: put, puts, putting — and the past is put again: yesterday I put it there. One of the easiest irregular verbs in English, and the chapter uses it as a drumbeat: put, put, put.
