faq-q: What does "will" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Will is the verb of the future. "It will come" puts the coming after now &mdash; and it is fixed: the coming is certain, and no chance is left in it. Will keeps one form for every person: I will, you will, he will, they will. It is word 18 of the 850 &mdash; and with it the book gets its firm line: word 17 opened a door of chance, and will says what walks through it. In the story the hero stops asking &mdash; he answers.
faq-q: What is the difference between "will" and "may"?
faq-a: May is chance; will is decision. "It may come" leaves the coming open &mdash; it may come, and it may not. "It will come" closes the question &mdash; the coming is fixed. In the story the two are the ask and the answer: in chapter 17 the hero said "May I?" and the world said "You may"; here the saying gets firm &mdash; "You will go" &mdash; and the hero takes it as his own: "I will go." May opens; will fixes.
faq-q: Why does "will" take no -s?
faq-a: Will keeps one form for every person: I will, you will, he will, she will, it will, we will, they will. There is no "wills" for the future and no changing &mdash; put will in front of any verb and the future is made: will go, will see, will send. Small words that never change carry the most weight in Basic English; may was one of them, and will is the next.
faq-q: What word comes after "will"?
faq-a: The next word is "about" &mdash; word 19 of the 850. About is the word of the middle: "a story about a cave" says what the story holds inside it. With will the hero has his future fixed; with about he gets the word for what stands round a thing &mdash; and the hidden story will want it, because what they sent him for is about to open.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With will the hidden story reaches its turn: they said "You will go," and for the first time he answered &mdash; "I will go." The permission was theirs (word 17); the future is his (word 18). What was given as leave, he takes as a promise.
