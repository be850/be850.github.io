faq-q: What does "seem" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To seem means to appear to be, to look like — without saying what really is. It is word 9 of the 850, and the story uses it to make the whole world uncertain.
faq-q: Why does the story turn doubtful exactly at word 9?
faq-a: By design. For eight chapters the hero got, kept, made and put things — a world of facts. Seem is the first word that says: maybe. The plot shifts from action to doubt, and the next two chapters (take, be) fight over what is real.
faq-q: What is the difference between "seem" and "be"?
faq-a: Seem reports appearance; be states reality. It seems true — but is it true? The book places seem at word 9 and be at word 11 on purpose: the question comes first, the answer two chapters later.
faq-q: Is "It seems" related to the dummy "it"?
faq-a: Yes. The same empty it from Chapter 1 — it comes, it gives — now runs the most doubtful sentence in English: it seems. Grammar gave the story its ghost back in Chapter 1; here the ghost starts talking.
faq-q: How do I use "seem" in my own sentences?
faq-a: Three easy frames: it seems + adjective (it seems true), it seems that + sentence (it seems that it comes), and subject + seems to + verb (he seems to go). All three appear in the chapter in miniature.
