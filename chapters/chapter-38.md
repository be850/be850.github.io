faq-q: What does "with" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: With is the word of the going with: a thing goes with a thing, and the going keeps them side by side. "I take the say with me" &mdash; the take puts the say with me; "the say goes with me" &mdash; it keeps with me in the going. It is word 38 of the 850 &mdash; the next word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gave his acts their order in time; between gave him the middle space; by named the doer and kept the near thing near; down took him down the line to the low place; from said where the coming starts; in put him inside; off took a thing away; on put it back; over took it over; through took it through; to took the act to its end; under put the keep under the send; up took the say up from under; with keeps the say with him.
faq-q: How does "with" work with the verbs?
faq-a: With goes with the verbs of taking, keeping, putting, coming and going. With take: "I take the say with me" &mdash; the take puts it with me. With keep: "I keep the say with me" &mdash; the keep holds it with me. With put: "I put the say with me". With come and go: "the say comes with me; I go with the say". With says the keep of the going: with me.
faq-q: What is the difference between "up" and "with"?
faq-a: Up and with are two sides of one take. Up says the going from under: "I take the say up" &mdash; the say was under, and the take puts it up. With says the keep after the take: "I keep the say with me" &mdash; it came up, and it keeps with me. In the chapter the say came up with the hero, and then it kept with him. A thing goes up; after the going, it goes with you.
faq-q: What word comes after "with"?
faq-a: The next word is "as" &mdash; word 39 of the 850. With keeps the say with the hero: it goes with him, he goes with it. As will say the being of one thing in the place of another: the say as his memory, the keep as his key. The hero has his subject (19), his road (20), his order (21), his push back (22), his place in the group (23), his point (24), the order of his acts in time (25), the middle space (26), the doer and the near thing (27), the low way (28), the start of the coming (29), the inside (30), the take-away (31), the put-back (32), the over (33), the through (34), the end of the act (35), the keep under (36), the going up (37), the keep with (38); the next word will say how one thing stands as another.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With under the fragments said where the say was kept and who put it up. With with they say the going with: they went with me, the say went with me, I was with him &mdash; and the one he sees beside himself was him. The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: the doer was always him; it was me.
