faq-q: What does "say" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Say is the verb of speech: to say a thing is to put it into words and let it go &mdash; to speak it so that another person has it. Its forms are say, says, saying and said. It is word 14 of the 850 &mdash; and with it the book gets a voice: the cave can finally talk back.
faq-q: What is the difference between "say" and "tell"?
faq-a: Say puts the words out; tell carries them to a person. You say a thing, but you tell somebody: I say "It is me"; I tell you it is me. Basic English takes say first &mdash; word 14; tell comes later in the list. Chapter 14 can quote speech, but nobody in it has a name yet, so there is nobody to tell.
faq-q: What does "said" give the story?
faq-a: Said is the past of say &mdash; words already out of the mouth. A said thing cannot be unsaid: it leaves the speaker and stays in the world. The memory fragment uses it for the turn of the hidden novel: the hero said "It is me," and they came. His own words were the cause &mdash; the saying was the making.
faq-q: How do quotes work with "say"?
faq-a: English marks spoken words with a colon and quotation marks: I say: "It is me." The marks are a gate &mdash; inside them the words are alive, spoken by a person, exactly as they were said. Chapter 14 is the first chapter of the book with dialogue: the hero speaks, and &mdash; for the first time &mdash; the others answer.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With say comes said, and the hidden story finds its trigger: I said it &mdash; I said "It is me" &mdash; and the saying made them come. What looked like chance begins to look like a summons.
