faq-q: What does "across" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Across is the word of the crossing: from this side to that side, over a thing and out on its far side. "I go across it" &mdash; I put the thing between my start and my end, and I come out on the other side of it. It is word 20 of the 850 &mdash; the second word of the list that is not a verb, after "about". About rounds a thing where it stands; across gives the road its far side. With about the hero got his subject; with across he gets the line from here to there &mdash; the first road of the book.
faq-q: How does "across" work with the verbs before it?
faq-a: Words 1 to 18 were acts, and "about" (word 19) rounds an act with its subject. Across adds the line of the crossing. Put it after a verb of moving and the move takes a start and a far side: "I go across it", "they come across it". Put it after "send" or "see" or "say" and the sending, the seeing, the saying reach the far side: "I send it across", "I see them across it". Basic English keeps its list small and lets the small words carry the roads; across, after, before, between will carry them all.
faq-q: What does "come across" mean?
faq-a: Two things, and the story uses both. "They come across" &mdash; they arrive from the other side; the coming crossed something. "Come across" may also point at a meeting on the way: you go about, and on the road you come across a thing &mdash; you meet it with no plan. In the chapter the hero says both: he goes across the line, and what he came across on it is the word itself.
faq-q: What word comes after "across"?
faq-a: The next word is "after" &mdash; word 21 of the 850. Across takes a thing between two sides; after takes a thing between two times: first this, then that. The hero has his subject (word 19) and his road (word 20); the next chapter gives his steps their order &mdash; and the hidden story, which sent the send across, will say what came after it.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With about the hidden story named its subject; with across it makes the crossing: they went across with the send, and the send came across &mdash; to him. The line between them and him was never empty; the it that crossed was him. They sent him across; he is the crossing coming back.
