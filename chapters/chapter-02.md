faq-q: What does "get" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Get is one of the most useful verbs in English: it can mean receive (get a gift), understand (I get it), and arrive (get here). It is word 2 of the 850.
faq-q: Why does the hero say "I get me?"
faq-a: In the story, to get a thing is to remember it. The hero gets everything around him — except himself. The question "I get me?" is the moment he notices the hole where his own memory should be.
faq-q: Is "I get it" different from "I understand it"?
faq-a: They mean the same in everyday English, but get is shorter and far more common in speech. Basic English prefers it because one small word does the work of many.
faq-q: Why does Chapter 2 repeat "I get it" so many times?
faq-a: Repetition is the engine of the book. Each line looks almost the same, but the cast changes: I get it, I get you, you get me, we get it. Your ear learns the pattern while your mind follows the plot — the two jobs become one.
faq-q: Can "get" really replace so many verbs?
faq-a: In daily English, yes: get a letter (receive), get a joke (understand), get home (arrive), get sick (become). Ogden put it at word 2 for exactly this reason. The story plays with all of these shades at once — which is why the chapter feels like a riddle.
faq-q: What does "The getting gets" mean?
faq-a: Again the gerund trick: the act of getting is treated as a thing that itself gets. It sounds like a tongue-twister on purpose — the book teaches you how English bends verbs into nouns before you ever see the word "noun".
