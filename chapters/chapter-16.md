faq-q: What does "send" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Send is the verb of distance: to send a thing is to make it go from you to a person or a place &mdash; it leaves you and arrives. Its forms are send, sends, sending and sent. It is word 16 of the 850 &mdash; and with it the book gets distance: what the hero holds can now leave him and arrive where he is not.
faq-q: What is the difference between "send" and "give"?
faq-a: Give needs near hands &mdash; the giver holds the thing and the taker takes it from that hand. Send does not need nearness: the thing travels. I give it; they get it. I send it; it goes, and they get it after. In the cave the hero gave what he could hold out; word 16 lets him put a thing on its way &mdash; even to where he cannot go.
faq-q: What does "sent" give the story?
faq-a: Sent is the past of send &mdash; a sending that already happened &mdash; and it cannot be taken back: what is sent is away from the sender. The memory fragment uses it for the origin of the hidden story: they said "Send it," and he sent it &mdash; and then they sent him. The hero was not a wanderer; he was a thing on its way &mdash; the sent was me.
faq-q: How do "send", "go" and "get" work together?
faq-a: A sending has two ends and a road between them: I send it &mdash; it goes &mdash; they get it. Send starts the going; go is the road; get is the arrival. Take away any one of the three and nothing was really sent &mdash; chapter 16 walks the whole chain so the reader can see each end.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With send comes sent, and the hidden story finds its origin: he did not come to the cave by chance &mdash; they sent him, the way one sends a thing. What looked like a beginning now looks like a delivery.
