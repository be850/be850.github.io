faq-q: What does "to" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To is the word of the going-to. It says where the act ends: "I go to the four" &mdash; the four are the end of my going; "the say goes to them" &mdash; they are the ones who get it; "I give the say to them" &mdash; I put it in their hands. It is word 35 of the 850 &mdash; the next word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gave his acts their order in time; between gave him the middle space; by named the doer and kept the near thing near; down took him down the line to the low place; from said where the coming starts; in put him inside; off took a thing away; on put it back; over took it over; through took it through; to takes the act to its end.
faq-q: How does "to" work with the verbs?
faq-a: To goes with the verbs of giving, going and coming. With go: "I go to the four" &mdash; the going has its end at the four. With give: "I give the say to them" &mdash; the say moves from my hands to theirs. With come: "they come to me" &mdash; their coming ends at me. With send: "I send it to them" &mdash; the send carries the say to them. To says the end of the act: no going, giving or coming is complete before it says its to.
faq-q: What is the difference between "to" and "from"?
faq-a: From says where the coming starts; to says where it ends. "The say is from me" &mdash; I am the start of it. "The say goes to them" &mdash; they are the end of it. In the chapter the say came from me and went to them, then came to me again: from is one end of the road, to is the other. A thing can be from me and to you at the same time &mdash; the two words hold the two ends of one act.
faq-q: What word comes after "to"?
faq-a: The next word is "under" &mdash; word 36 of the 850. To takes the act to its end; under will say where a thing is: lower than a thing, under it. The hero has his subject (19), his road (20), his order (21), his push back (22), his place in the group (23), his point (24), the order of his acts in time (25), the middle space (26), the doer and the near thing (27), the low way (28), the start of the coming (29), the inside (30), the taking-away (31), the putting-back (32), the going-over (33), the going-through (34), and the end of the going (35); the next word will take him under.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With off they took it away; with on they put it back; with over they took it over him; with through the say goes through him. With to they take the say to him: "They take the say to me... it was me." The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: it came from me; it was on me; it was over me; it goes through me; it comes to me.
