faq-q: What does "give" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: To give means to put a thing into someone's hands. English lets you say give someone something: I give you it. It is word 3 of the 850.
faq-q: Why does the book say "I give you it" and not "I give it to you"?
faq-a: Both are correct English. The book uses give you it because the little word to has not been learned yet — chapter 3 may only use words 1 to 3. The rule of the book is also a lesson in grammar: English has two ways to say the same gift.
faq-q: Who is giving the words to the hero?
faq-a: That is the mystery of Volume One. Every new word he gets seems to be given — but nobody around him says a word.
faq-q: Why does the chapter keep flipping "I give you it — you give me it"?
faq-a: The mirror pattern is the grammar lesson. English fixes the order: the giver goes first, the gift goes second, the receiver third. Swap the people and you swap the sentence. Saying it both ways around is how the pattern sticks without a single rule being named.
faq-q: Can "give" mean things other than objects?
faq-a: Yes: give time, give help, give a look, give it a go. English hands out this verb everywhere. In the story the hero gives and gives — and the strangest gift of the chapter is the question at the end: can a thing give itself?
faq-q: What is "The giving gives" doing in the story?
faq-a: The same gerund beat as the chapters before: the coming comes, the getting gets, the giving gives. By word 3 the drumbeat is a pattern you can hear — and patterns are what make a small vocabulary feel big.
