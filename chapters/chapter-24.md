faq-q: What does "at" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: At is the word of one point. A thing is at a place when it is there and only there &mdash; one place, one point, no group. "I am at the door" &mdash; I am not round about the house; I am at one point of it, and the point keeps me there. It is word 24 of the 850 &mdash; the sixth word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at takes him out of the middle and puts him at one point of it.
faq-q: How does "at" work with the verbs?
faq-a: Three ways. Put it with a verb of keeping, and the point holds the act: "They keep me at it; they kept me at it day after day." Put it with a verb of seeing, and the eye goes to the point: "I look at the door; I see them at the end." Put it with a verb of coming or going, and the road makes an end at the point: "The send came at me; I go at the door." Among says "in the middle of them"; at says "at this one point and no other".
faq-q: What is the difference between "among" and "at"?
faq-a: Among is many; at is one. "I am among them" &mdash; they are round about me, and I am one of the group. "I am at the door" &mdash; there is no group now: one point, one place, and I am kept at it. In the chapter they put him among them (word 23), and then they keep him at it (word 24): first the middle takes him in, then the middle puts him at one point of itself &mdash; the group is still round about him, but his place is one place now.
faq-q: What word comes after "at"?
faq-a: The next word is "before" &mdash; word 25 of the 850. Among and at are words of place: among is the middle, at is the point. Before is a word of time: it puts one act in front of another act &mdash; "I saw it before I said it." The hero has his subject (19), his road (20), his order (21), his push back (22), his place among them (23), and his point (24); the next chapter will ask what came before the point &mdash; and the hidden story will give him the answer.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With among they put him among them and said "You are the among." With at they keep him at it, at one point: they put him at the door, the say came at him from the door, and they said "You are the at. It is at you." The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: they kept me; I saw it from the point; it was me.
