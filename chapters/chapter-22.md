faq-q: What does "against" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Against is the word of the push back. One thing is against another when it goes the other way than that thing goes, or when it comes up to it and rests on it with a push. "They come against me" &mdash; they come my way, and my way is the other way. It is word 22 of the 850 &mdash; the fourth word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gives the line a push the other way.
faq-q: How does "against" work with the verbs?
faq-a: Three ways. Put it with a verb of going, and the road takes two sides: "I go against it" &mdash; it goes one way, I go the other. Put it with a verb of coming, and two things meet with a push: "the send comes against me" &mdash; it comes up to me and presses. Put it with an act you say, and the say takes a side: "I say it against them" &mdash; my say goes the other way than their say. After keeps the line of acts; against is what the line does when two acts will not go one way.
faq-q: What is the difference between "after" and "against"?
faq-a: After keeps the line; against pushes it. "I came after the send" &mdash; my coming took the next place in the line, the place the order gave it. "The send came against me" &mdash; the two of us met on the road, and each went its own way against the other. In the chapter the hero goes after the send, and the send comes against him: the order of his days (word 21) meets the push back (word 22) &mdash; and he stands.
faq-q: What word comes after "against"?
faq-a: The next word is "among" &mdash; word 23 of the 850. Against puts two things on two sides of a line; among puts a thing in the middle of more than two: "I am among them" &mdash; they are round about me, and I am one of them. The hero has his subject (word 19), his road (word 20), his order (word 21), his push back (word 22); the next chapter puts him among them &mdash; and the hidden story will say where they kept him.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With after the order came to light: the send went, and he came after his own send. With against the push back says its word: they said "It is against you," and he said "You are the against" &mdash; the send came against them, and he is what came after it and against it. They kept him; it was me; it is me.
