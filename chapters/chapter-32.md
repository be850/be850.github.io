faq-q: What does "on" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: On is the word of the putting-back. It says that a thing is put on a thing, or that a thing keeps going: "they put the say on me" &mdash; the say is on me again; "the send goes on" &mdash; it keeps going; "they keep it on me" &mdash; it is put on me and it stays. It is word 32 of the 850 &mdash; the next word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gave his acts their order in time; between gave him the middle space; by named the doer and kept the near thing near; down took him down the line to the low place; from said where the coming starts; in put him inside; off took a thing away; on puts it back.
faq-q: How does "on" work with the verbs?
faq-a: On goes with the verbs of putting and going. With put: "I put it on" &mdash; it was off, I put it back. With go: "the send goes on" &mdash; it does not go off; it keeps going. With keep: "they keep it on me" &mdash; they put it on me and it stays on me. With see: "I see it on me" &mdash; I see that it is on me. The word says where the thing is: on the thing, in its place.
faq-q: What is the difference between "on" and "off"?
faq-a: Off takes a thing away: "they take the say off me" &mdash; it is away from me. On puts a thing back: "they put the say on me" &mdash; it is on me again. In the chapter the say was off him, and they put it on him; but the say that came from him was in him the whole time. Off and on are the two sides: a thing goes off, a thing goes on.
faq-q: What word comes after "on"?
faq-a: The next word is "over" &mdash; word 33 of the 850. On puts a thing on a thing; over will take a thing over another thing: over the line, over the send. The hero has his subject (19), his road (20), his order (21), his push back (22), his place in the group (23), his point (24), the order of his acts in time (25), the middle space (26), the doer and the near thing (27), the low way (28), the start of the coming (29), the inside (30), the taking-away (31), and the putting-back (32); the next word will take him over.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With from the fragments named the start: "It came from me." With in the fragments put him inside his own act. With off the fragments showed the taking-away. With on the fragments put it back: "They put the say on me... it was me." The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: it was me; it came from me; I am in it; it is on me.
