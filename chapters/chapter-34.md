faq-q: What does "through" mean in Basic English?
faq-a: Through is the word of the going-through. It says that a thing goes in at one side and comes out at the other: "the say goes through me" &mdash; it comes in me and goes out of me; "I go through the send" &mdash; I come out on the far side of it; "the send goes through the four" &mdash; it goes from one end of them to the other. It is word 34 of the 850 &mdash; the next word in the line that began with "about". About gave the hero his subject; across gave him his road; after put his acts in their line; against gave the line a push the other way; among put him in the middle of the group; at took him to one point of it; before gave his acts their order in time; between gave him the middle space; by named the doer and kept the near thing near; down took him down the line to the low place; from said where the coming starts; in put him inside; off took a thing away; on put it back; over took it over; through took it through.
faq-q: How does "through" work with the verbs?
faq-a: Through goes with the verbs of going, taking and keeping. With go: "I go through the send" &mdash; I come in it and come out of it. With take: "they take the say through me" &mdash; they make it go through me. With keep: "they keep the say going through" &mdash; it does not stop. The word says the way of the act: in at one end, out at the other.
faq-q: What is the difference between "through" and "over"?
faq-a: Over takes a thing over a thing: "they take the say over me" &mdash; it passes over me, from this side to that side, and does not come in. Through takes a thing through a thing: "they take the say through me" &mdash; it comes in me and goes out of me. In the chapter the say was over him, then it went through him; he went through the send, through the four. Over is the way past the thing; through is the way from one side of it to the other.
faq-q: What word comes after "through"?
faq-a: The next word is "to" &mdash; word 35 of the 850. Through goes through a thing; to will say where a thing goes: from me to you, from one side to the other side. The hero has his subject (19), his road (20), his order (21), his push back (22), his place in the group (23), his point (24), the order of his acts in time (25), the middle space (26), the doer and the near thing (27), the low way (28), the start of the coming (29), the inside (30), the taking-away (31), the putting-back (32), the going-over (33), and the going-through (34); the next word will take him to.
faq-q: Why is part of the chapter in italics?
faq-a: Italics are memory. The hero recovers his past one fragment per chapter, told only with the words the book has given him so far &mdash; the fragments form a second, hidden story under the main one. With off they took it away; with on they put it back; with over they took it over him. With through the say goes through him: "They take the say through me... it was me." The hidden story keeps saying the same thing from a new side: it came from me; it was on me; it was over me; it goes through me.
